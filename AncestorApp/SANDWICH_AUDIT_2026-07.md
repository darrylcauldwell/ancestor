### DS-01 · high · (false-positive) No-age death/burial records auto-promote to .fact on a bare [15,100] plausibility band — father/son and any in-district namesake death applies to the tree

**Claim:** When the subject has no known death year (deathYearFrom nil, RecordScorer.swift:399) and the record carries no recorded age (recordedAge nil at :428-433 — true for ALL burial records, all FindAGrave memorials without dates parsed to age, and every FreeBMD death index row before 1866, which had no age column), the date gate passes iff the implied age-at-death range intersects [15,100] (:441-447). Name pass + local district + familyContext skip (:954 — death/burial have no family clause) = .fact with zero softFails (:93-94), and wouldApply(:29-31) writes the death year to the profile. For a subject with an exact birth year this accepts ~85 years of registrations of anyone with the same name in the district.

**Scenario:** John Smith Sr, b.1800 Belper (subject; death not yet known). His son John Smith, b.1830, lives in the same parish. The son dies Q2 1860; FreeBMD's pre-1866 death index row is just 'SMITH John, Belper, vol 7b' — no age. Scored for the father: implied age 60, inside [15,100] → date pass; surname/given exact → name pass; Belper isLocalDistrict → geography pass; familyContext skip. Verdict .fact. Applying the cluster writes death=1860 onto a man who lived until 1878 — and his post-1860 census records now score against a 'dead' subject (marriage/census-after-death then fires .impossible on his OWN later records via ScoringRules.validateRecord). Same mechanism fires for any same-name burial (FindAGrave, FreeREG) because burial isn't even in the district-extraction switch (:566-572) and 'Belper, Derbyshire' passes via the 'derby' literal (:606).

### DS-02 · high · (false-positive) Census wrong-household: ±5 year tolerance plus spouse-FORENAME household match makes the neighbour's family an affirmatively-confirmed .fact, and clustering will not split it back out

**Claim:** The census date gate accepts an implied birth year anywhere in window±5 (RecordScorer.swift:477-480, tolerance(for:.census)=5 at ScoringRules.swift:119). The familyContext household check then passes if ANY wife/husband-relation member's name scores ≥0.7 against the known spouse's name (:843-851) — but high-frequency Victorian forenames (Mary, Elizabeth, Sarah were together ~40% of women) plus the shared surname make the WRONG John Smith's household produce an exact 1.0 match. All four gates pass with no softFails → .fact, with the family gate actively endorsing the error. Downstream, ClusteringEngine only splits census contradictions when implied births differ by MORE than 5 (ClusteringEngine.swift:353-359), so the two households' records fuse into one life.

**Scenario:** 1881 census, Belper. Subject John Smith b.1848, wife Mary. Two streets away lives an unrelated John Smith b.1852, whose wife is also Mary. The wrong schedule scores: census birth 1852 inside 1848±5 → date pass; Belper → geography pass; household contains 'Mary Smith, wife' → nameSimilarity('MARY SMITH','MARY SMITH')=1.0 → familyContext PASS 'spouse Mary found in household'; verdict .fact. In clustering, implied births 1848 vs 1852 differ by 4 ≤ 5 → no split; the wrong household's children are now candidate children of the subject, and the two census records count as corroborating records for convergence (1 lineage, ≥2 records → .possible even before FamilySearch's copy of the same enumeration book adds a second 'lineage').

**Code:** `Ancestor Research/Services/Research/RecordScorer.swift:471-485`; `Ancestor Research/Services/Research/RecordScorer.swift:843-851`; `Ancestor Research/Services/Research/ScoringRules.swift:115-124`; `Ancestor Research/Services/Research/ClusteringEngine.swift:353-359`

**Verifier (CONFIRMED):** Every link in the claimed chain checks out against the actual code, and no existing mechanism rescues the scenario.

1) Date gate ±5 for census — CONFIRMED. RecordScorer.swift:477-480 passes when censusBirth is within [birthLow-tol, birthHigh+tol]; ScoringRules.swift:119 sets tolerance(.census)=5. A subject b.1848 (window 1848–1848) accepts a census-implied birth of 1852. The comment (lines 473-476) shows ±5 is a deliberate loosening from ±2.

2) Family gate wrong-household endorsement — CONFIRMED. RecordScorer.swift:843-851: pass if ANY household member whose relationship contains wife/husband scores nameSimilarity ≥0.7 against context.spouseName. ScoringRules.swift:211 gives exact 'MARY SMITH'='MARY SMITH' 1.0; even forename-only context 'MARY' vs 'MARY SMITH' scores 0.8 via containment (line 226). The wife check returns .pass immediately (line 850) — children are never consulted, and there is no negative signal for the household containing unknown children.

3) Verdict — CONFIRMED. RecordScorer.swift:91-94: all gates pass with no softFails → .fact. The thin-subject cap (lines 112-113) doesn't apply — the subject has a given name and a precise birth year. So the family gate actively converts what would otherwise be at most a lead into an affirmatively-endorsed .fact ('spouse Mary found in household').

4) Clustering won't split — CONFIRMED. ClusteringEngine.swift:353-357 splits census contradictions only when implied births differ by MORE than 5 (abs(latest-earliest) > 5); 1848 vs 1852 = 4 → fuse. The full split-rule set (findContradiction, lines 311-395) is: ≥2 births, ≥2 deaths, census >5 apart, ≥2 distinct marriage spouses. Notably there is NO rule that two census records from the SAME enumeration year (a physical impossibility for one person) force a split — two 1881 schedules coexist happily in one cluster.

5) Rescue mechanisms checked, none apply:
- BiographicalFitEvaluator: census records are corroboration-ONLY by design ('census mismatch does NOT apply plausibility penalty', BiographicalFitEvaluator.swift:116-117, near-miss note at :285); it evaluates birth-index candidates, not census records; and its chapman anchor (Belper=DBY both) is identical here anyway. Worse, Rule 4's ±2 corroboration means the wrong household's 1852 could corroborate a wrong birth candidate.
- ConvergenceEngine.swift:46: (1 lineage, ≥2 records) → .possible — the corroboration-inflation claim is exactly right; a FamilySearch copy of the same enumeration would add a second lineage (line 15: 'FreeBMD + FamilySearch are TWO lineages') pushing toward .probable.
- Multi-hypothesis .birthYearCandidate machinery targets tied precise birth-index candidates, not census household disambiguation.
- Apply-path overwrite policy would protect an existing precise birthDate field, but does nothing about the claimed harms: wrong-household .fact, wrong children as candidates, and inflated convergence.
- Name variants/strictness tiers are irrelevant — the names are identical.

The only softening note: ±5 census tolerance is a documented, intentional trade-off, and the ≤5 clustering threshold is deliberately symmetric with it. But the finding's point is the COMBINATION — loose date band + forename-level spouse confirmation + no same-year-census split — and that combination genuinely produces an affirmatively-confirmed wrong household that clustering cannot separate. The scenario (common name, common wife forename, same registration district, 4-year age gap) is realistic and unhandled.

**Verifier correction / scope:** Finding is accurate as stated. One precision: the household spouse check compares the full member name (not just forename) via nameSimilarity ≥0.7 — but since the wrong wife shares both forename and married surname, the effect is exactly as claimed (score 1.0), and even a forename-only context string still passes at 0.8 via substring containment.

> **PARTIALLY RESOLVED 2026-07-13 by the conflict layer (#CL-Change2, 36ca102)** — in-cluster same-enumeration-year impossibility check ⟨G13⟩: new `findContradiction` split trigger + sweep arm over persisted life_events, so two same-census-year records can no longer fuse or corroborate. Remains open (gate-repair): the ±5 tolerance + spouse-forename household endorsement that grades the wrong household .fact in the first place.

Both FreeBMD and FamilySearch are trustTier .transcription (2) and directness .directTranscription, so two copies give trustScore 4 → .probable, uncapped by the directness rule (:124-131).
